### **Maxwell Energy Systems x Bullz Racing** ###

As Bullz Racing prepares to take on a new challenge with our first-ever
electric vehicle for SUPRA SAEIndia 2027, we have been stepping into an
entirely new area of engineering. Moving from an internal combustion
powertrain to an electric one has meant learning, developing, and
rethinking systems that are fundamental to the way our car operates.

One of the most critical among the EV powertrain is the Battery
Management System (BMS).

For our team, the BMS is far more than an electronic component. It is
responsible for keeping a close watch over the battery by performing
functions such as cell voltage measurement, temperature monitoring,
current measurement, and error detection. With the battery being at the
heart of our new EV, having a reliable BMS and the right technical
support behind it has been essential.

This is where Maxwell Energy Systems has stepped in.

### **Technology That Fits Our Ambition** ###

![Maxwell BMS](Resources/Blogs/maxwellBMS1.jpg "class=float-right !w-[600px] !max-w-none !mx-0 !my-2 ml-8 mb-4")

As part of our EV development, Maxwell Energy Systems generously
sponsored us with their FSLT Legacy BMS along with its wiring harness.
What immediately stood out to us was how well the system aligned with
our requirements.

The FSLT Legacy BMS can monitor up to 25 cells in series. Our battery
architecture uses 24 cells in series, making the BMS an excellent fit
for our requirements while giving us the flexibility of an additional
channel.

Beyond cell voltage monitoring, temperature is another crucial parameter
when working with a high-voltage battery. The BMS allows temperature
monitoring through Negative Temperature Coefficient (NTC) thermistors.
This gives our team the ability to monitor individual cell temperatures
across the battery pack and understand its behaviour during development
and testing.

The system also gives us multiple ways to access the data we need.
Through isolated controller area network (CAN), the BMS can communicate
with an external device such as a laptop. At the same time, wireless
Bluetooth connectivity allows us to connect to the BMS through a mobile
phone.

And this is where the ION Lens application makes the experience
particularly convenient.

### **Making Complex Data Accessible** ###

![Maxwell ION Lens](Resources/Blogs/maxwellApp.JPG "class=float-left !w-[1000px] !max-w-none !mx-0 !my-2 mr-8 mb-4")

Through ION Lens, the parameters measured by the BMS can be monitored
directly from an Android device. Instead of requiring a complicated
setup, every time we want to examine battery data, our team can connect
wirelessly by simply scanning the QR code present on the BMS board.

For a student team working through the challenges of developing its
first EV, accessibility matters.

ION Lens is not limited to data visualisation either - the application
can be used to configure over 335 parameters, giving us a significant
level of control over the BMS and allowing us to work with the system in
a way that suits our development requirements.

It is this combination of capability and accessibility that has made the
FSLT Legacy BMS such a valuable part of our EV development process.
